# Data Breach Procedure

**Internal — Your Business Today Ltd — Version 1.0 — 24 September 2026**

What YBT does when personal data it holds may have been lost, stolen, seen by the wrong person, altered, encrypted or made unavailable. A client may ask to see this procedure.

## 1. What counts as a breach

A personal data breach is any security incident that leads to personal data being destroyed, lost, altered, disclosed or accessed without authority — by accident or on purpose. It includes:

- a client's database, backup or export reaching someone it should not;
- an account with access to client data being compromised;
- data sent to the wrong person or the wrong client;
- ransomware encrypting data, even if nothing is copied out — losing availability is a breach;
- a sub-processor telling YBT about a breach on its side.

If in doubt, treat it as a breach and follow this procedure. Deciding later that it was not reportable costs nothing.

## 3. The steps

**Step 1 — Contain (immediately).** Stop it getting worse: revoke the credentials or tokens involved, lock the affected accounts, isolate the affected resources, block the route in. Do not delete anything that might be evidence — take copies of logs first.

**Step 2 — Log it (within 1 hour).** Open an entry in the breach log (section 5) with what is known so far and the time YBT became aware. Update it as facts come in.

**Step 3 — Assess (within 12 hours).** Work out which client or clients are affected, what data, roughly how many people and records, whether the data was encrypted, and what the likely consequences are for the people concerned.

**Step 4 — Tell the client (within 24 hours of becoming aware).** YBT is the processor, so YBT does not report to the ICO about client data — the client does. But the client's 72 hours start when YBT tells them, so every hour YBT delays is taken from the client. Tell the client's named contact by phone and in writing, using the template in section 4, even if the facts are incomplete. Send updates as more is known.

**Step 5 — Help the client.** Give the client what it needs to decide whether to report to the ICO and whether to tell the individuals affected, and help with both if asked.

**Step 6 — Fix and review (within 10 Business Days).** Put right the cause, restore from backup if needed (and check that restored data does not bring back anything that had been deleted — see the suppression list in the Data Subject Requests procedure), and write a short review: what happened, why, and what changes so it does not happen again. Record it in the log.

**Where YBT is the controller** (its own staff, client-contact, billing or portal data), YBT itself reports to the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms, and tells the individuals without undue delay if the risk is high. Report online at ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach or by phone on 0303 123 1113.

## 4. Template notification to a client

>
> We are writing to tell you about a personal data breach affecting data we process for you, under Schedule 1 of our agreement.
>
> **When we became aware:** [date and time]
> **What happened:** [plain description]
> **Data affected:** [categories of data]
> **People affected:** [categories and approximate number]
> **Records affected:** [approximate number]
> **Likely consequences:** [what could happen to the people concerned]
> **What we have done:** [containment steps taken]
> **What we propose to do next:** [next steps]
> **Your contact at YBT:** Nigel Reilly, [phone], consulting@yourbusiness.today
>
> Your 72-hour period to report to the Information Commissioner, if the breach is reportable, runs from the time of this notice. We will help you with that and with any notice to the people affected. We will send updates as we learn more.
